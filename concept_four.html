Archival Synopsis

Set across the drought-scarred salt flats of Kutch and the austere brutalist corridors of post-independence Gujarat, Rahgeer chronicles an estranged classical archivist tasked with retrieving the lost nitrate reels of a banned revolutionary theater piece.

A meditation on the impermanence of national memory, tactile celluloid decay, and the burdens inherited by silent witnesses. Filmed entirely on Arricam ST with vintage Cooke anamorphic primes.

[04 // THE LINEAGE] THREE GENERATIONS • SEVENTY YEARS
GEN I // 1954–1980 01

The Architect

Krishnarao Marathe (1918–1989)

Founded Marathe Pictures in Ballard Estate following apprenticeships at Prabhat Film Company. Pioneered independent literary adaptations, collaborating with classical Marathi and Hindi dramatists. Replaced theatrical melodrama with naturalistic lighting and restrained spatial geography.

Key Works:

Agantuk (1956) • Devgandhar (1963) • Kinaara (1971)

GEN II // 1978–2010 02

The Rebel

Anand Marathe (1949–2016)

FTII gold medalist who spearheaded India's parallel cinema wave. Openly rejected song-and-dance formulas in favor of sync-sound location shooting, non-professional actors, and unflinching investigations into rural feudalism and institutional alienation. Honored with Venice & Locarno retrospectives.

Key Works:

Dhoop Chhaon (1982) • Pratighat (1988) • Mahanagar Raatein (1994)

GEN III // 2012–PRESENT 03

The Visionary

Devashish Marathe (Present Artistic Director)

Maintaining pure celluloid workflows in the age of algorithmic streaming. Blends tactile architectural framing with layered soundscapes and psychological international co-productions. 2024 recipient of the Satyajit Ray Vision Award for preservation and uncompromising directorial integrity.

Current Dossier:

The Salt Merchant (2018) • Rahgeer (2025 Cannes Competition)
